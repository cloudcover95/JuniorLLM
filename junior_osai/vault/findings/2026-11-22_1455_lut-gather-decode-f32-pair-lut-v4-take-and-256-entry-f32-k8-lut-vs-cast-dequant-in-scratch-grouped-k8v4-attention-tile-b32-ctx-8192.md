# LUT-gather decode for the scratch grouped K8V4 tile at B = 32 / ctx 8192: not worth it. A 256-entry f32 LUT for K8 is ~2.9x slower than the int8×scale dequant, and a 256 × 2 f32 pair LUT for V4 nibbles is slower than the u16-LUT take + cast it replaces; decode stays ~68–70% of the tile and attention stays above 50 ms

The 10:55 note ([[2026-11-22_1055_per-op-profile-of-scratch-grouped-k8v4-attention-tile-plus-v-scales-folded-into-p-and-tk512-inside-full-b32-decode-step-vs-scrd-vs-pool-ctx-2048-8192]]) asked: about 70% of the scratch tile is decode (K dequant ~24%, V4 nibble take ~23%, V scale ~22%). Can it be cut in half without touching error, by replacing the u16-LUT take + int8 view + cast with one np.take on a 256 × 2 f32 pair LUT straight into the group-major f32 V scratch (scales folded into P), and gathering K from a 256-entry f32 LUT and scaling with out=? Does decode drop below 50% of the tile, attention to ≤ ~50 ms at B = 32 / ctx 8192, and the step to a per-round low ≥ 1.5x vs pool at max abs ≤ 1e-5?

Answers:
- **No.** np.take with an f32 table writes 4 bytes per element through a gather, which is slower than numpy's contiguous int8→f32 cast-multiply. The f32 K LUT makes K decode **~2.9x slower** (serial 3.2–3.4 → 8.8–10.1 ms per 5-head task; attributed 15–18 → 45–55 ms). The V4 pair-LUT take costs 4.5–5.5 ms per task vs 2.5–2.9 ms for the u16 take, and saves only the cast/scale (DVL v_scale 0.64–0.76 vs DV 1.57–1.94 ms). Net V path: DVL 5.8 / 5.9 ms vs DV 4.8 / 4.1 ms per task.
- Decode share (K dequant + V take + V scale): D 68% / 67%, DVL 68% / 69%, LK 78% / 80%. It never drops below 50%.
- Pooled wall at B = 32 / ctx 8192 (W = 32, 8 threads, 15 calls): best new variant DVL 78.5 / 66.5 ms vs scrD 76.1 / 83.3 and scrDV 73.3 / 73.1. The pooled numbers are noisy on a shared box (run2 scrD 83 ms vs run1 76), but the single-thread serial task, which has no contention, is slower for every LUT variant on both seeds (DVL 0.95x / 0.90x of scrD, LK 0.62x / 0.65x). Attention ≤ 50 ms is not reached by any variant.
- **Error:** the K LUT and the row-major V pair LUT are bit-identical to scrD (max abs 0.0). The group-major fold is 2.4e-07 (q×1) / 9.5e-07 (large q×2) off scrD and ≤ 3.3e-06 vs pool. Error is fine; the speed is not.
- **Bytes:** unchanged. The LUTs are 1 KiB (K) + 2 KiB (V) per worker. KV + weights are 295.40 MiB at ctx 8192 (unchanged from 10:55). No memory win.
- The full step was not re-run: no variant beats scrD on attention, so the step can't beat the 10:55 scrD/scrDV step (1.60–1.75x vs pool).

## Verdict

**not-viable.** Neither LUT gather is a speedup. The K LUT is ~2.9x slower, and the V pair LUT is slower than the take + cast it replaces. Decode stays ~68–70% of the tile, attention is not ≤ 50 ms, and nothing is 1.5x better on speed or memory. Error is fine (≤ 3.3e-06 vs pool, including large q). Keep scrD (exact) / scrDV as the grouped tile. In numpy, a gather to f32 can't beat a contiguous int8→f32 cast; the decode cost is the f32 write itself, not the lookup.

## Next question

Is the scrD decode already at the memory-bandwidth roofline? At B = 32 / ctx 8192, time per tile: a plain f32→f32 np.copyto of the same tile bytes, a bare int8→f32 cast (no scale), and the scrD int8×scale out= dequant, all on the 8-thread pool. If dequant is within ~1.2x of the f32 copy, numpy has no decode lever left, so stop tuning the attention tile and move the lane back to the 2-bit weight side (qkv/o/gate-up/down are ~50% of the step at ctx 2048). Otherwise, the gap shows the headroom for a fused K8V4 dot product.
